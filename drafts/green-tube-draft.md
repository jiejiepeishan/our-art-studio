# Seat A — PG7 engine (and its teal cousin)

**History once:** Phthalo green **PG7** is chlorinated copper phthalocyanine, sold as pigment from **1935** (Monastral). Twin of yellow-shade **PG36** (Helio). Cousin of phthalo **blue** PB15. Industrial ink/car-paint strength that watercolor inherited. Complements pyrrol/scarlet into chromatic black (your learning note). Does **not** behave like viridian.

### 12. Winsor & Newton · Green Gold ⭐  
`wn-tube-green-gold` · **PY129** · #8A9A3A · 5ml · transparent · ⭐

**temp_role**  
`Warm-cool green-gold · Copper azomethine (PY129) · yellow that reads green · mixer · not a foliage crayon`

**ace_note**  
W&N Professional Green Gold — classic British workhorse in 5ml.

**This is a yellow with a green alias.** **PY129** (copper azomethine / Irgazin / “green gold”) reads mossy yellow-green in masstone and behaves like a **muted transparent yellow** in mixes. Not PG7. Not sap. Not viridian. Painters buy it for glowing low-chroma greens, lichen, late sun on grass, and mixes that stay alive without going highlighter.

DS’s **Rich Green Gold** is the same index; DS also sold a different “Green Gold” historically — trust **PY129**.

**Play lab:** Masstone vs a long dilute. If dilute goes gold-yellow and masstone goes olive, that’s PY129. Then touch it into ultramarine (you’ll get a range of mosses) — that’s *why* it’s a mixer, not a Mix Lab shopping list.

**Dual advice:** **one PY129 seat.** Nothing else in the green family replaces it. Don’t treat it as a fourth sap. vs nickel azo PY150 (inside Sap/Undersea): PY150 is a glowing gold-yellow; PY129 is greener, more moss. Teammates, not twins.

**ace_history**  
PY129 is a late-20th-century copper-azomethine pigment — industrial yellow-green that artists nicknamed green gold. W&N’s card text (“rich gold yellow with green undertones”) is more honest than the family tag *green*. Turner-era “green gold” lakes were different, fugitive stories; this is the modern lightfast stand-in.

### 13. Winsor & Newton · Terre Verte ⭐  
`wn-tube-terre-verte` · catalog **PG23** · W&N listings often **PG23 + PG18 + PB28** · #6B8A6A · 5ml · granulating · ⭐

**temp_role**  
`Muted mineral clay-green · Green earth (celadonite/glauconite) · verdaccio / quiet flesh underpaint · weak on purpose`

**ace_note**  
Soft mineral green — underpainting and quiet foliage.

**The weakest honest green in the tin — that’s the point.** Genuine **PG23** is green earth: **celadonite / glauconite**, used since Rome and Pompeii frescoes, the verdaccio under flesh in Renaissance painting. Notoriously **low tinting strength**. Landscape distance, olive drab, a whisper under skin. It will not fight Phthalo. It should not try.

**Check the tube.** Some current W&N Professional Terre Verte listings add **PG18 + PB28** (viridian + cobalt blue) because pure PG23 is *too* weak for modern customers. If you see three indices, this is a boosted earth, not peasant clay. Catalog single **PG23** may be optimistic.

vs **Cobalt Deep PG26**: manufactured spinel mutter, more body. vs **Viridian PG18**: chromium glass, cooler, stronger than earth. Terre verte is the dirt that is actually green.

**Play lab:** A wash that looks like “nothing happened.” Then glaze a warm flesh or ochre over it. If the passage goes alive in a dusty way, you understand verdaccio. If you wanted a leaf, you opened the wrong tube.

**Dual advice:** **one green-earth seat.** Don’t stack boosted W&N + a later genuine single PG23 unless the swatches disagree loudly. Not a mixer engine. Not sap.

**ace_history**  
Green earth is among the oldest greens still sold: marine clays, iron in the silicate, fresco-safe. Renaissance workshops greened the shadows of faces with it before pink. Modern “terre verte hues” are often phthalo soups — W&N’s better versions still start from PG23. The weakness is the history lesson.
